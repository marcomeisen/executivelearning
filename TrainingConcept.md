# Executive Technology Training for Claims-Insurtech Leaders

## Building Strategic Technology Understanding for Claims Platforms, Automation & Ecosystems

---

# 1. Executive Learning Objectives

## After the training, participants should understand:

### Technology & Claims Operating Models

* How a modern end-to-end claims journey works across insurer, customer, adjuster, repair network, TPA, and third-party partners
* Where technology creates measurable operational value — and where human expertise remains essential
* The difference between:

  * digitizing a manual process
  * automating decisions
  * creating scalable claims operations

### Platform & Architecture Thinking

* Why platform architecture directly impacts:

  * growth
  * speed of customer onboarding
  * integration costs
  * scalability
  * valuation multiples
* The business implications of:

  * monolithic systems
  * modular platforms
  * ecosystem architectures
* Why APIs and integrations are strategic capabilities, not IT details

### Data & AI in Claims

* Why claims data is a long-term strategic asset
* How AI actually creates value in claims:

  * fraud detection
  * triage
  * document extraction
  * straight-through processing
* The operational and legal limits of AI in regulated claims environments

### Executive Decision-Making

* How to evaluate Build vs. Buy decisions realistically
* How vendor lock-in happens
* Why scaling from one insurer to multiple enterprise clients changes technology requirements dramatically
* How governance and organizational structure influence innovation speed

---

## Typical Executive Misconceptions the Training Should Correct

### Common Misconceptions

* “Digital claims” automatically means “automated claims”
* Cloud migration alone creates scalability
* AI can replace operational expertise
* Faster development always means better technology decisions
* Integrations are primarily technical problems
* Claims platforms scale like eCommerce platforms
* Buying software reduces strategic complexity
* Cybersecurity is mainly an IT responsibility

---

## Better Questions Participants Should Learn to Ask

### Platform & Architecture

* “What business flexibility does this architecture enable or limit?”
* “How difficult is onboarding a new insurer or partner?”
* “Where could vendor dependency become strategic risk?”

### Automation & AI

* “Which claims decisions are truly automatable?”
* “What data quality assumptions does this AI model depend on?”
* “How do we detect false positives or biased outcomes?”

### Scaling & Operations

* “What breaks operationally if claim volumes triple?”
* “Where are the hidden manual processes?”
* “What operational dependencies still require human intervention?”

### Product & Governance

* “Who owns product decisions versus delivery decisions?”
* “What slows innovation more: technology or governance?”
* “How do we prioritize between customer customization and platform scalability?”

# Module 1 — Claims Operations & Technology Fundamentals

## Objective

Create a shared understanding of how claims organizations operate and where technology meaningfully improves outcomes.

## Core Topics

* End-to-end claims lifecycle
* FNOL (First Notice of Loss)
* Triage and routing
* Assessment and settlement
* Vendor coordination
* Human workflows vs. machine workflows
* Digitization vs. automation

## Executive Translation

* Scanning PDFs is not automation
* A digital portal without workflow intelligence still creates manual work
* Technology creates most value at operational bottlenecks and handoffs

## Claims-Insurtech Relevance

Executives learn where automation creates:

* lower loss adjustment expense
* faster settlement
* better customer experience
* fraud reduction

---

# Module 2 — Claims Platforms & Architecture Without Technical Jargon

## Objective

Understand how platform structure affects growth, scalability, partnerships, and cost.

## Core Topics

* Monolith vs. modular platform
* Best-of-breed ecosystems
* APIs explained through claims examples
* Integration economics
* Multi-tenant vs. customer-specific solutions

## Executive Translation

* Architecture determines operational agility
* Every insurer customization creates future complexity
* APIs are business connectors, not technical buzzwords

## Claims-Insurtech Relevance

Critical for:

* onboarding carriers
* integrating repair networks
* enabling ecosystem partnerships
* scaling internationally

# 5. Suggested Training Framework

## Recommended Structure

### Phase 1 — Self-Learning Foundation

Duration: 2–4 weeks

Short executive-friendly modules:

* 20–40 minutes each
* focused vocabulary and concepts
* minimal technical depth
* business examples only

### Phase 2 — Executive Workshops

Format:

* remote or on-site
* 2–4 hour sessions
* interactive discussion based
* decision-oriented

### Phase 3 — Applied Reflection

Participants apply concepts to:

* current platform strategy
* vendor landscape
* operating model
* investment priorities

### Phase 4 — Executive Review Session

Final strategic discussion:

* key risks
* platform maturity
* scaling constraints
* organizational implications

---

## Recommended Principles

### Focus on:

* business impact
* operational consequences
* strategic trade-offs
* technology as operating leverage

### Avoid:

* coding
* infrastructure detail
* engineering terminology overload
* theoretical architecture discussions

# Core Topics

---

## 1. From Projects to Products

### Executive Explanation

Traditional insurers often think in:

* projects
* implementation milestones
* delivery deadlines

Modern Insurtech organizations think in:

* products
* continuous improvement
* long-term platform evolution

### Claims Example

A claims portal is never “finished.”

It continuously evolves based on:

* customer behavior
* fraud patterns
* operational bottlenecks
* insurer requirements
* regulatory changes

## 2. Agile Organizations Explained Simply

### Executive Explanation

Agile does NOT mean:

* lack of planning
* chaotic execution
* endless meetings

Agile means:

* shorter feedback cycles
* cross-functional ownership
* faster learning
* incremental delivery

### Claims Relevance

Claims environments constantly change:

* catastrophe events
* fraud patterns
* partner ecosystems
* customer expectations

Agile organizations adapt faster to operational realities.

Executives often push for:

* slows onboarding
* increases maintenance cost
* reduces scalability

One insurer-specific FNOL workflow can create:

* operational exceptions
* integration complexity
* future upgrade limitations

* inconsistent product strategy
